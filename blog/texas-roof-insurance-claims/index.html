Filing a Roof Insurance Claim in Texas: A Complete Homeowner's Guide

January 2026 · Waterloo Roofing Co

Texas leads the nation in property insurance claims related to roof damage, and for good reason. Between hailstorms, high winds, and the occasional tornado, Central Texas roofs take a beating. Filing an insurance claim can feel overwhelming if you have never been through the process, but understanding each step will help you get the coverage you deserve and avoid costly mistakes that delay or reduce your payout.

Step 1: Document the Damage Immediately

As soon as it is safe to go outside after a storm, begin documenting your property. Your insurance company will want evidence, and the more thorough your documentation, the stronger your claim will be.

  • Photograph everything: Take wide-angle shots of your entire roof from the ground, then close-up photos of any visible damage to shingles, gutters, vents, skylights, and flashing. Include images of damage to other parts of your property, such as fences, siding, window screens, and outdoor AC units, since these help establish the storm's severity.
  • Record the date and time: Note exactly when the storm occurred. Cross-reference this with National Weather Service reports for your zip code, which document hailstone size, wind speeds, and storm paths.
  • Save any damaged materials: If pieces of shingle or flashing are on the ground, keep them. Physical evidence supports your claim.
  • Check interior spaces: Look for water stains on ceilings, damp insulation in the attic, or moisture around window frames. Interior damage strengthens the case for a full roof replacement rather than a partial repair.

Step 2: Contact Your Insurance Company

Most Texas homeowner's insurance policies require you to report damage "promptly," though the exact timeframe varies by carrier. As a general rule, file your claim within 30 days of the storm if possible. Texas law gives you up to one year to file for weather-related damage under most policies, but waiting too long can complicate things, especially if additional storms cause overlapping damage.

When you call your insurer, have your policy number ready and provide a clear, factual summary of the damage. Ask for a claim number and the name of the adjuster assigned to your case. Request a timeline for when the adjuster will inspect your property.

Step 3: Get an Independent Roofing Estimate

Before the insurance adjuster visits, schedule an inspection with a licensed local roofing contractor. A reputable roofer will provide a detailed written estimate that itemizes the damage, materials needed, and labor costs. This independent assessment serves as your baseline. If the adjuster's estimate comes in significantly lower, you have documentation to support a negotiation or supplemental claim.

Your roofing contractor can also be present during the adjuster's inspection. This is a standard and perfectly acceptable practice in Texas. Having a knowledgeable professional on your side ensures that all damage is identified and properly scoped, including items that an adjuster unfamiliar with roofing systems might overlook, such as compromised underlayment, damaged drip edge, or weakened decking.

Step 4: Working with the Insurance Adjuster

The adjuster works for your insurance company, but their goal is to accurately assess the damage within the terms of your policy. Here is how to make the process go smoothly:

  • Be present for the inspection: Walk the property with the adjuster and point out all areas of damage you have documented.
  • Share your documentation: Provide copies of your photos, weather reports, and your roofing contractor's estimate.
  • Ask questions: If the adjuster excludes certain damage or classifies something as "wear and tear" rather than storm damage, ask for a specific explanation. You have the right to understand every line item.
  • Review the scope carefully: After the inspection, you will receive a written scope of loss. Compare it line by line with your contractor's estimate. Discrepancies in square footage, material quantities, or missing line items are common and can be addressed through a supplement request.

Texas-Specific Rules You Should Know

Texas has several laws that protect homeowners during the insurance claims process:

  • Prompt Payment Act: Under Texas Insurance Code Chapter 542, your insurer must acknowledge your claim within 15 days, begin investigating within 15 days after that, and pay or deny the claim within 5 business days of reaching a decision. If they miss these deadlines, you may be entitled to an 18% annual penalty plus attorney's fees.
  • Right to appraisal: If you and your insurer cannot agree on the damage amount, most Texas policies include an appraisal clause. Each side hires an independent appraiser, the two appraisers select an umpire, and the majority decision is binding. This is faster and cheaper than litigation.
  • No "assignment of benefits" restrictions: In Texas, you can assign your insurance benefits to a roofing contractor, though this is generally not recommended unless you fully understand the implications.

Common Mistakes That Hurt Your Claim

Avoid these pitfalls that we see Austin homeowners make regularly:

  • Waiting too long to file: Damage worsens over time. A small leak from storm damage can turn into mold and structural rot if left unaddressed for months.
  • Making permanent repairs before the inspection: Temporary measures to prevent further damage (tarping a leak) are fine and expected. But replacing shingles or making permanent repairs before the adjuster sees the damage can void your claim.
  • Signing a contract with a storm chaser: Out-of-town contractors who appear right after a storm often push homeowners into signing contracts with cancellation penalties. Work with an established local company that will still be here next year.
  • Accepting the first offer without review: The initial estimate is a starting point, not a final answer. Legitimate supplemental claims are a normal part of the process.
